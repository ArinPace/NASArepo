# Running GO / NO-GO in a classroom

One lesson of 45 to 50 minutes, ages 12 and up. Nothing to install: open the
game in a browser on a shared screen or one laptop per team.

## Set up (5 minutes)

- Teams of two to four. On the start screen, add a player for each student
  and type their names. Each student now owns a console: Mission Design,
  Spacecraft, Science, or Comms and Data.
- Choose **Witness** for a first game. Leave the level on **Cadet**.
- Tell the class the true part: on 13 April 2029 an asteroid 340 metres wide
  passes closer to Earth than our television satellites, and space agencies
  are planning missions to meet it.

## Play (25 minutes)

- Pitch and route: 7 minutes. Ask each team to say out loud why they picked
  their launch date.
- Design: 8 minutes. Rule for the room: nobody adds a part without saying
  what it will cost somebody else.
- Test and launch: 4 minutes. Each console owner votes and gives a one-line
  reason. A NO-GO is allowed and is not a failure.
- Fly: 6 minutes. When the game stops for a call, the console owner decides
  after hearing the team.

## Debrief (15 minutes)

Put one team's debrief on the shared screen and use the "Why it happened"
list.

1. Which decision cost the most? Which one saved the mission?
2. Was the result bad luck or a bad design? Look at the 1,000 flights.
3. If you could change one thing and fly the same world again, what would it
   be? Then do it, and read the flight history.
4. Where did you have margin, and where did you spend it?
5. When did somebody vote NO-GO, and were they right?

## Going further

- Switch to **Engineer** or **Chief engineer** to see the numbers and the
  equations behind each meter.
- Try **Last call**: the same asteroid with a year less to prepare.
- Try **Ice Hunter**: no launch window at all. Ask why the battery is now
  the part that can end the mission, and look up the eclipse of 31 December
  2028.
- Press "Run the checks" on the start screen and ask why the model's Apophis
  flyby is three hours off NASA's time.
- Compare your design with ESA's real Ramses mission in the debrief.

## What students should leave with

- A launch window is geometry, not a choice.
- Every kilogram is paid for several times over.
- Tests cost schedule, and schedule is a resource.
- Margin looks like waste until the day it is needed.
- A team that can say NO-GO is safer than one that cannot.
